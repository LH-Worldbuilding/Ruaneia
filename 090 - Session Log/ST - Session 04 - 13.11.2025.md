## The Search for Puritas
The Characters head out in a search for [[Puritas]], the blade forged by [[Harlon]], said to be capable of cutting through anything. The last known location of [[Puritas]] is in an [[Ash-Lake]] on the way north to the capital of [[Montriviére]].

### Exit of Daniel Vannier
Room Five: Resolution

**WIS (Perception)**:
- **DC 15**: notice tattoo
- **DC 20**: see the depiction of the tattoo

As [[Daniel Vannier]] takes his leave, you notice a before unseen tattoo, stretching halfway up the right side of his neck.
The tattoo depicts a raven looming over a ruined tower, a sign of allegiance to the Lord/Lady of [[Ravencourt]], [[Dominique Carménère]]. These tattoos are also often used to cover up bitemarks.

### Refugee Caravan
Room One: Entrance (and/or Puzzle)

On their travels, players encounter a group of refugees, headed towards [[Vinecrest]]. They fled from [[Lestrine]], an old monastery looking over the [[Ash-Lake]]. Displaced by mercenaries and deserters camping in a hill fort west-north-west of [[Lestrine]].

**Brother Caelric:** a middle aged human monk, with a thin build.
Uprooted by mercenaries, he leads the caravan, of some seven people, south. However what he is truly fleeing is the increasing influence of [[Atroxitoplas]] near the Capital of [[Montriviére]] to the north.
With him he carries a rusty sickle, which he refuses to use for combat or protection and a small wine casket sealed with silver strings. He claims the wine is of the last batch he overlooked and holds sentimental value as a remembrance. In actuality, the wine had been enchanted by the [[Elyndra|Lady of the Dawning Fields]], to glow in the presence of great evil.

### Ash-Lake
Room Three: Setback

Ash played an important role in the cultivation of [[Montriviére]]'s vineyards, stored in vast lake like basins known as [[Ash-Lake]]'s throughout the province, it could quickly be accessed and distributed. Now, with the increase in ash from burned corpses, fields and villages that came with the revolution, the [[Ash-Lake]]'s have lost their purpose, only standing as a testimony to a bygone age of agricultural growth and nurturing.
